# The 30-Day Money Reset
## A No-Bullshit Guide to Taking Control of Your Finances

**By D.C. Cleary**

---

### Introduction: Why Most Financial Advice Is Useless

Most personal finance books are written by people who've never been broke. They tell you to skip lattes and invest in index funds while you're trying to figure out how to pay rent. They talk about "delayed gratification" to people who've been delaying everything their entire adult lives.

This isn't that book.

I wrote this because at one point I was staring at €23,000 in debt with no clear way out. The standard advice wasn't helpful. "Cut back on discretionary spending" means nothing when you've already cut everything. "Make a budget" doesn't help when your income barely covers your fixed costs. "Invest in your future" is absurd when you're worried about next week.

What worked wasn't one big change. It wasn't a lucky break or a sudden windfall. It was a system — a 30-day reset that changed how I thought about money, not just how I spent it.

This book is that system. No judgment. No lectures. No "just stop being poor" advice. Just what actually works when you're starting from where you actually are.

### PART ONE: WEEK 1 — FACE THE NUMBERS

#### Day 1: The Full Inventory

Most people don't know how much debt they have. They know it's "a lot" or "too much" but they've never written it all down in one place. There's a reason for this: it's terrifying.

Do it anyway.

Open a blank document or grab a notebook. Write down every single debt you have. Credit cards. Loans. Overdrafts. Money owed to family. Buy-now-pay-later balances. Everything.

For each one, write:
- Who you owe
- Total balance
- Interest rate
- Minimum monthly payment
- Due date

Don't judge yourself. Don't calculate totals yet. Just write it down. The simple act of putting it on paper takes it from an amorphous cloud of anxiety to a concrete problem. And concrete problems have solutions.

#### Day 2: The Real Budget

Now the harder part: what you actually spend.

Not what you think you spend. Not what you should spend. What the bank statements say you spent.

Go through the last three months of transactions. Every single one. Categorize them: rent, utilities, food, transport, subscriptions, entertainment, debt payments, everything.

Most people discover they're spending 30-50% more than they thought on "small" things. The €3 coffee isn't the problem. It's the five subscriptions you forgot about. The takeaway that became routine. The "just this once" purchases that happen three times a week.

#### Day 3: The Leak Finder

Subscription creep is real. Go through every recurring payment:
- Streaming services
- Gym memberships
- Software subscriptions
- Phone apps
- Delivery passes
- Magazine subscriptions
- Cloud storage
- Anything that charges you monthly

For each one, ask: "Did I use this in the last 30 days?" If the answer is no, cancel it. If yes, ask: "Is this worth what it costs?" If you hesitate, cancel it. You can always resubscribe later.

The average person finds €50-150/month in forgotten subscriptions. That's €600-1,800 per year. Tax-free. Zero effort. Immediately available.

#### Day 4: The Income Map

Now the other side of the equation.

List every source of money you have access to:
- Primary job (after tax)
- Any side work
- Benefits or allowances
- Regular gifts from family
- Tax refunds
- Anything else

Then — and this is the important part — list five ways you could add €100/month within the next 30 days. Not "maybe someday." Specific, concrete actions you could take this week.

Examples:
- Sell five items you don't use
- Offer a skill you have (writing, design, tutoring, manual labor)
- Pick up one evening of delivery driving
- Rent out something you own
- Cancel and switch a service to a cheaper provider

#### Day 5: The Emergency Question

Here's the question that changes everything: if you lost all income tomorrow, how long could you survive at your current spending level?

For most people, the answer is measured in days, not months. This isn't meant to scare you — it's meant to motivate you. Every euro you save, every debt you pay off, every extra income stream you build extends that timeline.

Your goal by the end of this 30 days: extend that timeline by at least two weeks. By the end of the year: at least one month.

#### Day 6: The Negotiation Day

Pick up the phone. Call every provider you have:
- Internet
- Mobile phone
- Insurance (car, home, health, life)
- Bank (account fees, overdraft charges)
- Any service with a monthly bill

Say this exact phrase: "I'm reviewing my budget and looking to reduce my costs. Is there anything you can do to lower my monthly bill?"

That's it. Don't threaten to leave. Don't make up competitor offers. Just ask.

Most companies have retention discounts they're authorized to offer but only if you ask. You'll save 10-30% on at least half of these calls. For a 20-minute time investment, you can save hundreds per year.

#### Day 7: Rest, Review, Reset

You've done the hard part. You've faced the numbers. You've found the leaks. You've started plugging them.

Today: don't do anything about money. Go for a walk. See friends. Live your life. The rest of the month is about action. Today is about being proud that you started.

### PART TWO: WEEK 2 — STOP THE BLEEDING

The money you don't spend is more valuable than the money you earn. Every euro saved is:
- Tax-free
- Effort-free (once the system is set up)
- Immediately available
- Not dependent on anyone else

This week isn't about earning more. It's about spending less — permanently.

#### The Grocery Audit

Food is most people's second-biggest expense after housing. And it's the one with the most waste.

For one week, track every food purchase. Every supermarket trip. Every takeaway. Every coffee. Every snack.

At the end of the week, calculate:
- What percentage was groceries vs prepared food?
- How much did you throw away?
- What did you buy that you already had?

The average household wastes 20-30% of their food budget. Fixing this alone saves €50-150/month.

#### The Transport Rethink

Transport is typically the third-biggest expense. Ask yourself:
- Could you bike, walk, or use public transport for some trips?
- Is your car more expensive than it needs to be?
- Are you paying for parking you could avoid?
- Could you carpool or share journeys?

Even small changes add up. One less tank of petrol per month is €60-100.

#### The Entertainment Pivot

You don't need to stop having fun. You need to stop paying full price for it.

- Free museum days
- Happy hour instead of full-price drinks
- Home cooking instead of restaurants (invite friends over — it's cheaper and more social)
- Library instead of bookshop
- Nature instead of paid attractions

The goal isn't deprivation. It's being intentional about where your money goes.

#### The Housing Question

Housing is usually 30-50% of income. It's also the hardest to change quickly. But ask yourself:
- Could you get a roommate or lodger?
- Could you negotiate your rent?
- Could you move to a cheaper area when your lease is up?
- Could you house-sit or sublet temporarily?

These are big decisions, not quick fixes. But even a €200/month reduction in housing costs is €2,400/year.

### PART THREE: WEEK 3 — BUILD THE ENGINE

Now we flip from defense to offense. Saving money has limits — you can only cut so much. Earning money has no ceiling. This week is about creating new income.

#### Day 15: The First €100

Your mission: make €100 this week from something that isn't your main job.

- Sell something you own
- Do a one-off gig
- Offer a skill to someone who needs it
- Complete a micro-task
- Provide a service in your neighborhood

The amount doesn't matter. What matters is proving to yourself that you can generate money when you need to. Once you've done it once, you know you can do it again.

#### Days 16-21: The Side Hustle Sprint

Pick one income-generating activity and do it every day for a week.

If you're selling items: list 3 new things daily.
If you're freelancing: pitch 5 potential clients daily.
If you're creating content: publish one piece daily.

Volume beats talent at this stage. You don't need to be the best. You need to be consistent.

#### Day 22: The System

Whatever made you money this week — document it. Write down every step. Create templates. Automate what you can. The goal is to make it repeatable without requiring your full attention every time.

This is the difference between a one-off cash injection and a sustainable income stream.

### PART FOUR: WEEK 4 — PROTECT AND GROW

#### The Money System

By now you should have:
- A clear picture of your debts
- A realistic budget
- Canceled subscriptions saving you money
- At least one additional income source
- A documented system for repeating it

This week is about making it permanent.

#### Automate Everything

Set up automatic payments for:
- Rent and utilities (never miss these)
- Minimum debt payments (protect your credit)
- Savings (even €10/week — it's the habit that matters)

Automate the boring stuff so you can focus on the important stuff: earning more and paying down debt.

#### The Debt Snowball

There are two approaches to paying off debt:

1. **Avalanche Method:** Pay minimums on everything, throw all extra money at the highest interest rate debt first. Mathematically optimal.

2. **Snowball Method:** Pay minimums on everything, throw all extra money at the smallest balance first. Psychologically better — you get quick wins that motivate you to continue.

Pick whichever one you'll actually stick with. The best debt repayment strategy is the one you don't quit.

#### The 6-Month Plan

Where do you want to be in six months? Pick ONE financial goal. Not five. One.
- Debt paid off
- Emergency fund built
- Income stream established
- Deposit saved
- Credit score improved

Write it down. Put it somewhere you'll see daily. Tell someone who'll hold you accountable.

#### Day 30: The System

Money management isn't about willpower. It's about systems. You've built yours over the last 30 days. Now maintain it.

**Weekly:** Review spending, check progress on goals, adjust as needed.
**Monthly:** Review all subscriptions, negotiate any bills coming up for renewal, update your debt balances.
**Quarterly:** Reassess income streams, look for new opportunities, celebrate progress.

### The 10 Money Rules

1. Spend less than you earn. Everything else is commentary.
2. Debt is a mathematical problem, not a moral failing.
3. The best investment is the one you actually make.
4. Negotiate everything. The worst they can say is no.
5. Cash in the bank is freedom. Everything else is speculation.
6. Your income is your biggest wealth-building tool. Protect it and grow it.
7. Automate the boring stuff. Save your willpower for the hard decisions.
8. Comparison is the thief of financial progress. Run your own race.
9. Small changes compound. €50/month saved is €18,000 over 30 years.
10. It's never too late to start. The best time was 10 years ago. The second best time is today.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Should I pay off debt or build savings first?**
Build a small emergency fund first (€500-1,000). Then attack debt. Once high-interest debt is gone, build a proper emergency fund (3-6 months expenses).

**What if I can't make the minimum payments?**
Contact your creditors immediately. Most have hardship programs. Ignoring the problem makes it worse. Facing it, even when it's scary, is the first step to fixing it.

**How do I start investing?**
Clear high-interest debt first. Build an emergency fund. Then start with low-cost index funds. Don't pick individual stocks. Don't chase crypto gains. Slow and steady wins.

**What's the one thing I should do right now?**
Write down every debt you have. Every balance. Every interest rate. Face the numbers. Everything else flows from there.
