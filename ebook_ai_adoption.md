## A Practical Guide to Getting Started With Artificial Intelligence

**By Dylan Cleary**

### Introduction

Every business leader knows they should be doing something with AI. The emails from vendors are relentless. The board is asking questions. The competitors are announcing AI initiatives.

But most of what's sold as "AI strategy" is either too vague to be useful or too technical to be actionable. This book is neither.

I spent four years designing and deploying AI inside a global enterprise. I've seen what works and what wastes money. This guide gives you the framework to make real decisions about AI — not just talk about it.

### Chapter 1: AI Is Not Magic

Artificial Intelligence is pattern recognition at scale. It's very good at:
- Processing large amounts of text or data
- Finding patterns humans would miss
- Automating repetitive decisions
- Generating content based on examples

It's terrible at:
- Understanding context it hasn't been trained on
- Making ethical judgments
- Explaining why it made a decision
- Knowing when it's wrong

The companies that win with AI understand both columns. They use AI for what it's good at and keep humans for the rest.

### Chapter 3: The Build vs Buy Decision

**Build when:**
- It's your core competitive advantage
- You have unique data that off-the-shelf tools can't use
- You need full control over the AI's behavior
- You have engineering resources available

**Buy when:**
- It's a common business function (email, CRM, accounting)
- The vendor has better data than you
- You need it working this quarter, not next year
- You don't have AI engineers on staff

**The Hybrid Approach:**
Most companies should buy 80% and build 20%. Use off-the-shelf AI for commodity functions. Build custom AI only where it creates real competitive advantage.

### Chapter 5: Your First 30 Days

**Week 1: Audit**
- List every manual, repetitive task in your business
- Score each by: time spent, error rate, customer impact
- Identify the top 3 automation candidates

**Week 2: Research**
- For each candidate: is there an off-the-shelf AI tool?
- Get demos. Ask about EU AI Act compliance.
- Compare pricing and integration effort.

**Week 3: Pilot**
- Pick ONE tool. Start with the simplest, highest-ROI option.
- Run a 2-week trial with a small team.
- Measure: time saved, errors reduced, customer satisfaction.

**Week 4: Decide**
- If the pilot worked: scale it.
- If it didn't: kill it. Try the next candidate.
- Document what you learned. AI adoption is iterative.

---

### Chapter 6: How Not to Waste Money

- Don't hire an "AI team" before you know what you need
- Don't build custom AI when an off-the-shelf tool exists
- Don't pay for "AI strategy" that doesn't name specific tools and timelines
- Don't buy AI because a competitor did
- Do start small, measure everything, and kill what doesn't work
