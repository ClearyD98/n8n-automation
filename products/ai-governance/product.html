The Irish Regulatory Landscape

Key Authorities

  • Data Protection Commission (DPC): National supervisory authority for EU AI Act enforcement in Ireland. Same body that fined Meta €1.2B. They're not messing around.
  • Enterprise Ireland: Government agency supporting Irish business — potential funding source for AI readiness projects (Digitalisation Voucher, Operational Excellence Offer)
  • National Standards Authority of Ireland (NSAI): Will issue Irish-specific AI standards aligned with CEN/CENELEC
  • Competition and Consumer Protection Commission (CCPC): Consumer-facing AI applications fall under their remit

National AI Strategy: "AI - Here for Good" (2024)

Ireland's strategy explicitly calls for: - AI adoption across enterprise (pillar 2) - Trustworthy AI governance (pillar 1) - Public sector AI procurement standards

This creates a regulatory push-pull: companies need AI to compete (government wants adoption), but must comply with the AI Act (government will enforce).

EU AI Act Deadlines

Date Requirement Who's Affected
2 Feb 2025 Prohibited AI practices ban Everyone
2 Aug 2025 GPAI model rules (transparency, copyright) AI model providers
2 Aug 2026 High-risk AI system rules (Annex III) Companies deploying high-risk AI
2 Aug 2027 GPAI systemic risk obligations Large AI model providers
2 Aug 2030 High-risk AI from regulated products Medical devices, machinery, toys

August 2026 is the big one. That's when most Irish companies with operational AI need to be compliant.

5-Phase AI Governance Maturity Model

Phase 1: Unaware

  • No awareness of AI Act requirements
  • Using AI tools without governance
  • No AI inventory exists
  • Risk: Maximum — fines apply from Day 1 of enforcement
  • Action: Emergency assessment needed

Phase 2: Reactive

  • Aware AI Act exists but no action taken
  • May have informal AI usage policies
  • No documentation, no risk classification
  • Risk: High — cannot demonstrate compliance
  • Action: Fast-track framework establishment

Phase 3: Defined

  • AI inventory completed
  • Risk classifications assigned
  • Basic governance policies documented
  • Staff aware of obligations
  • Risk: Medium — framework exists but untested
  • Action: Operationalize and monitor

Phase 4: Managed

  • Regular AI audits conducted
  • Conformity assessments documented
  • Human oversight mechanisms active
  • Incident response procedures in place
  • Risk: Low — compliant with core requirements
  • Action: Continuous improvement

Phase 5: Optimized

  • AI governance integrated into business strategy
  • Proactive horizon scanning for regulatory changes
  • AI ethics board or designated officer
  • Internal AI literacy program
  • Risk: Minimal — best-in-class
  • Action: Maintain and share best practices

EU AI Act Risk Classification

Prohibited AI (BANNED from Feb 2025)

  • Subliminal manipulation
  • Exploitation of vulnerabilities (age, disability)
  • Social scoring by public authorities
  • Real-time remote biometric identification in public (with narrow exceptions)
  • Predictive policing based on profiling
  • Emotion recognition in workplace/education
  • Biometric categorization inferring protected characteristics

Check: Are you doing any of these? → Stop immediately. Fine exposure: up to €35M or 7%.

High-Risk AI (COMPLIANCE REQUIRED by Aug 2026)

Annex III categories: 1. Biometrics (remote identification, emotion recognition in some contexts) 2. Critical infrastructure (transport, energy, water) 3. Education and vocational training (admissions, assessment) 4. Employment and worker management (recruitment, promotion, termination, task allocation, monitoring) 5. Access to essential services (credit scoring, insurance pricing, benefits eligibility) 6. Law enforcement 7. Migration and border control 8. Administration of justice and democratic processes

Irish industries heavily affected: Banking/fintech (#5), insurance (#5), recruitment (#4), education (#3), utilities (#2), legal (#8)

Requirements for high-risk AI: - Risk management system - Data governance (training data quality, bias detection) - Technical documentation - Record-keeping (automatic logging) - Transparency and user information - Human oversight - Accuracy, robustness, cybersecurity - Conformity assessment - Registration in EU database - CE marking (if applicable)

Limited-Risk AI

  • Chatbots, emotion recognition (non-prohibited contexts), deepfake detection
  • Requirement: Transparency — users must know they're interacting with AI
  • Fine for non-compliance: up to €15M or 3%

Minimal-Risk AI

  • AI-enabled video games, spam filters, inventory management
  • No specific obligations beyond general product safety law

Assessment Methodology

Phase 1: AI Inventory (Week 1)

  1. Department-by-department survey — What AI tools are in use? Who approved them? What data do they process?
  2. Shadow AI detection — ChatGPT subscriptions, free AI tools, browser extensions, API-accessed models
  3. Vendor assessment — Which third-party tools have AI features? What's in the Terms of Service?
  4. Document all use cases — Purpose, data processed, decision impact, human oversight level

Phase 2: Risk Classification (Week 1-2)

  1. Map each AI system to EU AI Act risk tier
  2. Document the classification rationale — Why is this system high-risk/limited-risk/minimal-risk?
  3. Flag borderline cases — Systems that could be reclassified (e.g., an HR tool that's currently not used for termination decisions but COULD be)
  4. GDPR intersection analysis — Where does AI data processing overlap with data protection obligations?

Phase 3: Gap Analysis (Week 2)

  1. Score against each compliance requirement (0-4 scale)
  2. Calculate risk exposure — "If the DPC audited you tomorrow, what's the potential fine?"
  3. Prioritize gaps — Critical (fine risk) → High (compliance failure) → Medium (best practice) → Low (nice to have)
  4. Estimate remediation effort — Hours, cost, timeline per gap

Phase 4: Framework Design (Week 2-3)

  1. Governance structure — AI officer role, reporting lines, board oversight
  2. Policy suite — AI usage policy, data governance policy, vendor AI assessment policy
  3. Process design — AI procurement approval workflow, incident response procedure, audit schedule
  4. Documentation templates — Technical documentation format, conformity assessment template, risk register

Phase 5: Implementation Support (Week 3-4)

  1. Pilot implementation — Stand up framework for 1-2 high-risk systems
  2. Team training — AI literacy session for relevant staff
  3. Handover — Framework documentation, ongoing monitoring schedule
  4. 3-month check-in — Review framework effectiveness (Full package only)
