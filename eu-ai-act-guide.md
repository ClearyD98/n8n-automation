## About This Guide

The EU AI Act is the world's first comprehensive AI regulation — and it takes full effect in August 2026. If your Irish business uses AI in any capacity — hiring, customer service, pricing, logistics, fraud detection — you're almost certainly in scope.

This guide cuts through the 400+ pages of legislation and gives you exactly what you need: what applies to you, what you need to do, and how to do it without spending €50,000 on Big 4 consultants.

## Chapter 1: Does the AI Act Apply to You?

**Short answer: Probably yes.**

The EU AI Act applies to any business that:
- Develops or deploys AI systems in the EU market
- Has AI systems whose output is used in the EU
- Is established outside the EU but its AI output affects people in the EU

For Irish businesses, this covers virtually every company using:
- AI-powered recruitment or HR tools
- Automated customer service chatbots
- AI-driven pricing or underwriting
- Predictive maintenance systems
- Fraud detection algorithms
- AI-generated content or marketing

**The key test:** Does an AI system make or influence decisions that affect people? If yes, you're in scope.

## Chapter 2: The Risk Tiers

The AI Act classifies AI systems into four risk tiers:

### Unacceptable Risk (BANNED)
- Social scoring systems
- Real-time biometric identification in public spaces (with limited exceptions)
- AI that manipulates human behavior to cause harm
- Emotion recognition in workplaces/schools

**You almost certainly don't build these. If you do, stop immediately.**

### High Risk (REGULATED)
This is where most Irish businesses will fall. High-risk AI includes systems used in:

**Regulated sectors:**
- Employment and HR (recruitment, promotion, performance monitoring)
- Essential services (credit scoring, insurance underwriting)
- Education and training
- Law enforcement and border control
- Critical infrastructure (energy, transport, water)

**Product safety:**
- Medical devices with AI components
- Machinery, toys, lifts, PPE with AI safety functions

**If your AI falls here, you need:**
- Risk management system
- Data governance and quality controls
- Technical documentation
- Record-keeping (logging)
- Transparency and user information
- Human oversight
- Accuracy, robustness, and cybersecurity measures

### Limited Risk (TRANSPARENCY)
- Chatbots (must disclose they're AI)
- AI-generated content (must be labeled)
- Emotion recognition systems
- Deepfake generation

### Minimal Risk (NO REGULATION)
- AI-powered video games
- Spam filters
- Inventory management
- Most internal business process automation

## Chapter 3: The €250 Audit — Your First Step

Before spending anything on compliance, you need to know what you're dealing with. Here's the exact process:

### Step 1: AI System Inventory (2-3 hours)
List every AI system your company uses or builds. For each one, document:
- What does it do?
- What data does it use?
- Who does it affect?
- Was it built in-house or bought from a vendor?
- Does it make decisions autonomously?

### Step 2: Risk Classification (1-2 hours)
For each system, determine its risk tier using the framework in Chapter 2. Be honest — under-classifying is worse than over-classifying.

### Step 3: Gap Analysis (2-3 hours)
For each high-risk system, check:
- [ ] Do we have a documented risk management process?
- [ ] Is our training data documented and quality-controlled?
- [ ] Do we have technical documentation for the system?
- [ ] Are we logging system operations?
- [ ] Do users know they're interacting with AI?
- [ ] Is there a human who can override decisions?
- [ ] Have we tested for accuracy and bias?
- [ ] Do we have a cybersecurity assessment?

### Step 4: Prioritized Action Plan (1-2 hours)
Rank gaps by:
1. Regulatory risk (what gets you fined first)
2. Business impact (what affects customers most)
3. Implementation effort (what's fastest to fix)

## Chapter 5: What Happens If You Don't Comply

The fines are substantial:
- **Up to €35 million or 7% of global annual turnover** (whichever is higher) for banned AI practices
- **Up to €15 million or 3%** for most other violations
- **Up to €7.5 million or 1.5%** for incorrect information to authorities

For an Irish SME with €5M annual revenue:
- 7% = €350,000
- The cost of compliance (€250-€5,000) is roughly 1-2% of the potential fine

**The math is simple: compliance is cheaper than non-compliance by a factor of 70-100x.**

## Chapter 6: Irish-Specific Considerations

### The DPC (Data Protection Commission)
Ireland's DPC will be the lead supervisory authority for many tech companies' AI Act compliance. They're well-funded and increasingly aggressive on enforcement.

### Interaction with GDPR
The AI Act doesn't replace GDPR — it adds another layer. If your AI processes personal data, you need BOTH GDPR and AI Act compliance. The good news: GDPR documentation often covers 40-50% of AI Act requirements.

### Semi-State and Public Sector
If you sell to Irish government agencies or semi-states, AI Act compliance will become a procurement requirement. Getting ahead now is a competitive advantage.

### The Skills Gap
EY's 2026 Tech Leaders Outlook Survey found 36% of Irish tech leaders cite skills shortage as their #1 barrier — up from 24% in 2025. External AI expertise is in high demand.
